The financing discussion has changed since this article was first published in September 2020. The old blanket claim that ADU mortgage financing was unavailable was incorrect as current guidance and has been removed.

[Fannie Mae's ADU guidance](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/originating-underwriting/mortgage-products/accessory-dwelling-units) says its Selling Guide loan products can support purchases of homes with ADUs, renovations, or additions of an ADU. It identifies HomeStyle Renovation and construction-to-permanent financing among the options. This does not mean every borrower, property, or proposed project qualifies, or that every lender offers each option.

## Start with the project and your existing loan

Tell lenders whether you are buying a property, converting existing space, or building a detached unit. Ask which plans, permits, valuation, and contractor documents they require. If you already have a mortgage, compare keeping it with any proposal that replaces it. A new rate on the whole loan balance can matter more than the rate on the ADU portion alone.

## Home-equity loans and HELOCs are different

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-home-equity-loan-and-a-home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc-en-247/) describes a home-equity loan as a lump sum and a HELOC as a line that can be drawn repeatedly up to a limit. Home-equity loans may carry fixed or adjustable rates; HELOCs usually have adjustable rates. When there is an existing mortgage, these can be additional second-mortgage obligations.

Ask how the payment changes when the draw period ends. Check fees, minimum draws, interest-only periods, repayment terms, and whether a rate increase would strain the household budget. Borrowing against a home puts that home at risk if payments are not made.

## Compare the full financing plan

Request written terms for the same project scope. Put loan amount, cash contribution, fees, interest, draw schedule, repayment start, and any conversion or refinance requirements side by side. Ask what happens if construction runs late or the completed appraisal comes in below expectations.

Do not assume projected ADU rent will count toward qualification. Ask the lender to explain the applicable rental-income rules and documentation in writing. Planning cash flow and qualifying for a mortgage are different tasks.

## Cash funding still has a cost

Using savings avoids an ADU loan payment but reduces liquid reserves. Keep household emergency funds separate from construction contingency. Consider how much cash would remain before the unit earns rent and whether selling investments would have tax consequences.

## Test a difficult year before choosing

Run a case with later occupancy, lower collected rent, and higher borrowing costs where the loan permits rates to change. Include property operating expenses and the existing mortgage. A low advertised monthly payment is not enough to establish affordability.

Take the result to a lender and a qualified adviser. This article is education, not a financing offer or approval. Read [the cash-flow comparison guide](/blog/adu-cash-flow-with-and-without-adu/) before treating rent as the amount available for debt service.
